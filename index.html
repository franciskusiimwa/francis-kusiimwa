How I think

02 · Seven rules I keep
01

Understand the problem before you fix it.

Uganda has had a credit bureau since 2008, and most adults still aren't in it. If the problem with lending were only missing information, the bureau would have fixed it by now. So with IDM-B we started from the daily work of small lenders, how they find borrowers, decide, collect and keep records, and built it with them.

02

Get the assumptions right before betting on them.

Every plan rests on assumptions, and the dangerous ones are usually the ones nobody has written down. I name them early and find out, as cheaply as I can, whether they hold. I want each one as close to right as time and money allow before anything big depends on it. I learned this at iCreate Hub, which ran out of cash before it reached the scale its model needed.

03

Research isn't finished until someone can use it.

A study that only ends up in a journal changes very little. I care that findings get absorbed into the product and the field, and the work is done when there's something people can use, tried out with the same people who told us what they needed.

04

Don't take money you can't earn.

At IDM-B we turn down deals, whatever they pay, if we can't solve the client's real problem. That's why our first meetings are mostly us asking questions.

05

Turn everyone into a product manager.

The person at the front desk sees problems I never will. If everyone on the team learns to spot a problem, test a fix and measure it, the whole company gets smarter, and I stop being the bottleneck.

06

AI can drive. People choose where to go.

Running a driving school taught me a lot about the future of work. Machines are getting very good at the driving. Four things stay with us.

07

Use AI to create value, with your eyes open.

I believe AI is a force for good, and the people who use it to create real value will come through this wave. I use it every day and build AI tools that help my teams get sharper. I'm not an evangelist, though. Building sensitive software quickly with AI and without proper checks can open serious security holes.

03

Things I'm still figuring out

I don't have settled answers to most of these. They keep coming up, whatever I'm working on.

  1. 01The one I ask most

    How do we build excellent businesses that truly care about what customers need, and still make money?

    Too many businesses around me don't truly solve the customer's problem. The food is off, the pharmacy layout makes no sense, or the owner who finally gets customers turns difficult. Most of the fixes don't need a reinvention. And where the care is there, the economics are often off. I want to get both right.

  2. 02Root causes

    What's the reason behind the reason?

    If I can see a gap in the market, I'm probably not the first. There's usually a reason it's still open, and it's often political or social, not only commercial. I want to understand that reason before I try to fill it.

  3. 03Motives

    What really drives people to do what they do?

    What people say and why they act are often different things. Understanding the real motive changes how you design, sell and lead.

  4. 04Nonprofits

    How can a nonprofit pay its own way without losing the vision it started with?

    Sustainability has to be designed in from the start. I think impact only lasts when the bottom line is solid.

  5. 05Africa

    What would it take for businesses in or from Africa to truly compete globally?

    I don't like "built for Africa" as a goal. Too often it becomes an excuse for a lower bar. I'd rather build to a standard that would hold anywhere, and localise along the way.

05 · Teams and coaching

I'd rather build a team that thinks for itself than one that waits for me.

2 vs 0.5sign-ups per TikTok live against a regular video
One of the team's experiments at Platinum

TikTok lives against TikTok videos.

The team wanted to know which brought in more learners. Over two weeks they ran both. Each live brought in about 2 sign-ups, against about 0.5 per video, even though the videos reached more people over a longer time. The smallest live still drew 351 viewers. It was a small, two-week test, but the gap was wide enough to take seriously. The team made lives one of Platinum's main marketing channels, run from the company account and from staff accounts with big followings.

At Platinum Driving School I've coached everyone, permanent and part-time, to think like product managers. They spot new ways to add value for customers, design small experiments and run them. My job is mostly to say yes, and for small budgets they usually don't need to ask.

I use the same approach across all my startups. At Educate! I work closely with the delivery teams in the schools, because a product is only as good as the people who deliver it.

What I shut down

06 · And what it cost
Mokachi Labs · 2023 to 2024

The parts cost more than the business could carry.

We started as consultants, solving each client's problem one at a time. That broke on parts: good suppliers had minimum orders far bigger than any one client needed. So we tried building products in bulk, and the market was too small to buy them.

I was one of three co-founders and worked on it full-time. We worked until about 2am most nights for the whole life of the company. Part of the failure was mine: I wanted to move at a pace we couldn't sustain.

Now I work out what each unit costs to make and deliver before I fall for the product, and I set a pace the team can keep.

iCreate Hub · 2019 to 2022

We bet on assumptions we never checked.

Our business model only worked at scale. We assumed we'd reach that scale in time. We didn't, and we ran out of cash before we got there. I had put in about $5,000, I tried and failed to raise more, and honestly I didn't know how.

The money wasn't the hard part. I was 23, and I had to let go of 8 people, some of them with young families. I kept imagining what they would tell their families. It took a real toll on me.

This is where rule 02 comes from. I now name the assumptions a plan depends on, and test the ones that could kill it first.
